Out-of-pocket health spending remains a major driver of financial hardship in India, particularly among households facing chronic and acute disease burdens. This study examines the composition of out-of-pocket expenditure and the relative weight of different cost components—such as medicines, diagnostics, consultation, hospitalization, and transport—in determining the economic burden of illness. By identifying the major cost drivers across disease categories, the paper highlights how health spending patterns shape household vulnerability and catastrophic health expenditure. The findings are relevant for designing strategies that improve financial protection, reduce avoidable medical costs, and strengthen equitable access to healthcare in India.
