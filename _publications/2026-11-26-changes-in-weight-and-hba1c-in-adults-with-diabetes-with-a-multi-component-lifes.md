Background: Change in weight and HbA1c after a multi-component lifestyle programme added to metformin, and its relation to baseline adiposity and glycaemia, are unknown in lower socio-economic industrial workers with diabetes. Methods: Retrospective cohort in a hospital-based lifestyle clinic in India. Adults with long-standing diabetes and &gt;=2 visits were included; metformin was prescribed at standard dose by protocol (dose/adherence not recorded). Programme: plant-based diet following the 21-point traffic-light guideline, daily four-spice infusion, Yoga with pranayama, seven-step protocol; adherence assessed by calls. Weight and HbA1c change (first to last; negative = fall) analysed. STROBE reporting. Results: Of 140 patients, 114 had paired weight, 75 paired HbA1c; median age 50, 73 (52.1%) women. Baseline BMI 26.4 (SD 5.2) kg/m2, HbA1c 7.39% (SD 2.33). BMI &gt;=25 in 54.5% (72/132), &gt;=30 in 17.4% (23/132), lean (&lt;23) in 23.5%. Median follow-up 88 days for weight (range 1-465) and 84 for HbA1c (1-596). Weight fell 0.97 kg (95% CI 0.45-1.50), largely in overweight patients; in contrast, the leanest gained 0.19 kg. HbA1c fell 0.56 points (0.02-1.11), concentrated in &gt;=9.0%. Among 62 (44%) hypertensive patients, systolic/diastolic pressure fell 3.4/2.5 mmHg, with no significant association with HbA1c fall. Baseline BMI and HbA1c uncorrelated (r=-0.06, p=0.56), as were changes (rho=-0.05, p=0.67). 18.4% (21/114) patients lost &gt;=5% weight; 40.0% (16/40) with baseline HbA1c &gt;=7% reached &lt;7%; 10.0% (7/70) achieved both a &gt;=5% weight loss and &gt;=1.0-point HbA1c fall. Conclusions: In these lower socio-economic industrial workers, diabetes was common in lean workers; the add-on lifestyle programme was associated with weight loss in overweight patients and an HbA1c fall overall. The observed changes were concentrated in patients with raised baseline BMI or HbA1c. Without a comparator, efficacy and the contribution of any single component, including the spice infusion, cannot be determined.
