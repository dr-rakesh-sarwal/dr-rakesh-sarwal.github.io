This study estimates the burden of child undernutrition for smaller electoral units in India, revealing that aggregate-level analyses may hide important local disparities. By examining variation at substate and constituency-level geographies, the paper demonstrates that undernutrition is not evenly distributed and that vulnerable populations may be concentrated in specific regions. The findings are relevant to designing targeted nutrition strategies, improving accountability in program delivery, and directing resources toward communities with the greatest need. In doing so, the paper strengthens the case for more geographically precise public health planning in India.
